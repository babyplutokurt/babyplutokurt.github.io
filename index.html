Taolue (Kurt) Yang

Ph.D. Candidate in Computer Science, Temple University

I build systems that make AI and data-intensive science faster and cheaper by removing redundancy: compressing data, lowering numerical precision, and avoiding repeated computation, while keeping results exact or within guaranteed error bounds. I work mostly on GPUs, but also on TPUs and CPUs.

I am advised by Prof. Sian Jin and work closely with Prof. Xinghua (Mindy) Shi on genomic data. Along the way I have interned twice at Google and once at Oracle, and my changes to NVIDIA's NeMo-RL and ModelOpt were merged upstream. Outside of work, you will usually find me skiing.

Research

Compression-native genomic computing Sequencing and pangenome data grow faster than the hardware to analyze them. I build GPU compressors for this data (CuVert-Q, GFAz, GPUFASTQLZ, SeqBench), run analytics directly on compressed pangenome graphs, and, most recently, share exact computation across thousands of personal genomes when running deep-learning models, making inference up to 584× faster than standard batched execution with identical outputs.
Quantization for efficient LLM training and inference I have worked on reduced-precision LLM systems across hardware: int8 inference kernels on AMD CPUs at Oracle, LLM serving on TPUs and GPU quantization at Google, and error-bounded compression of KV caches and embeddings in my Ph.D. work. Next, I am interested in quantization-aware RL post-training, FP8/FP4 fine-tuning, and error-bounded KV-cache quantization.
